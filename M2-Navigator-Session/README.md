# M2: Navigator Session

*Explain a complex technical concept in 60 seconds.*

**Target Skills: Verbal Precision, Concise Summarization, Accessible Communication**

## Task

Description of task.

I recorded a 60-second video of myself explaining one technical concept clearly, with no filler words and no notes. The constraints:

- Exactly 60 seconds. Not 50, not 90. The constraint is part of the challenge.
- No notes. I planned it before hitting record, but didn't read.
- No filler words. I tried replaced "um," "uh," "like," "kind of" with pauses.
- No screen-share. Talking head only. This was meant to be an explanation, not a presentation.

## Process

- I picked my concept and planned a rough arc (hook, definition, example, why it matters) without scripting it word for word.
- I practiced once or twice without recording, noticing where I reached for fillers and trying to replace them with silence.
- I recorded a few takes, keeping it to three or four since after that the concept stops feeling natural.
- I uploaded the final take.

## Deliverable

Link to video: [https://youtu.be/1yaJR_h6m3o](https://youtu.be/1yaJR_h6m3o)

In the video, I introduce recursion with a clone analogy, then solve 2 to the 3rd power to demonstrate how recursive functions work and how base cases are used. I end by explaining recursion in simple, non-technical terms. By structuring the explanation this way, I let viewers understand recursion through three different lenses.

## Reflection

In Module 2 of TLA, I was paired with a peer from this cohort and took part in a pair programming session where we both experienced the Driver and Navigator roles. These statements reflect my experience and what I learned from working collaboratively during the session.

**Before the session, I predicted that Driver would be the harder role. After the session, was that true? What was harder (or easier) than I'd expected?**

I found that being the Driver was actually easier than expected. It felt natural to narrate my actions and check that my partner was following along. The Navigator role turned out to be the harder one, specifically, finding the right moment to interrupt the Driver to flag a logical error without disrupting their flow.

**Before the session, I flagged one anti-pattern I was most at risk of falling into (Drifting Apart). Did it show up? If yes, did I catch myself? If no, did a different one show up instead?**

It did show up briefly. I got distracted by notifications, which caused me to drift. I caught myself, muted my notifications, and apologized to my partner so we could refocus and complete our task.

**I named one strategy for the 5-second rule moments. Did it work? What actually happened the first time I noticed my Driver making a mistake?**

The first time the Driver made a mistake, I struggled to interrupt and waited too long before pointing it out. However, in the corrections that followed, I gave her space to think it through first rather than jumping in immediately or waiting too long, which showed that I trusted her thought process.

**One thing about my partner I want to remember: what she did well, what she struggled with, or how she reacted to me.**

My partner excelled as the Driver. She clearly communicated her thought process as she coded, kept her work readable, and structured it in a way that I could easily build on.
